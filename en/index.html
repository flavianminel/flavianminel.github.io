Historian of the early modern economy, postdoctoral researcher at FRAMESPA (UPVD). My thesis (2025) shows how the Estates of Languedoc developed a political economy at the provincial scale: they financed royal woollen manufactories and sought to govern flows of capital and raw materials (1670–1789). In the ANR project PRICk, I trace the genealogy of the consensus behind French and European plastics regulation since 1970, reconstructing the coalitions of industrialists, experts and policymakers.

Research

My approach starts from flows: capital, raw materials, labour, skills, norms. I follow them through provincial records (receipts of the Treasurer of the Bourse and deliberations of the Estates), royal records (Contrôle général, Navy, Council of Commerce), and notarial and manufactory records (Castanier, Flottes, Villeneuvette and Pennautier papers). The receipts, examined from 1682 to 1787, are structured in a relational database published in open access.

Wool industrialisation and the Old Regime economy

How the Languedoc textile sector was organised (1670–1789): circulation of capital, wool and labour; the role of manufacturers, financiers and local elites in the face of provincial and royal manufacturing policy.

A political economy at the provincial scale

The Estates of Languedoc devised their own economic policy: funding for manufactories, infrastructure and regulation, negotiated with royal power. Eighteenth-century political economy was also made in the provinces.

Territories and Mediterranean circulation

Production and export networks linking Languedoc, Marseille and the Levant; mediation between local, provincial and royal scales.

Thesis

2021 – 2025
defended 27 Nov. 2025

Structurer l'industrie, stabiliser les flux, gouverner le territoire : le cas de l'industrie lainière languedocienne (1670–1789)

PhD in early modern history, UPVD, FRAMESPA
Supervisors: Marc Conesa and Stéphane Durand

How did a province regarded as peripheral become one of the kingdom's leading wool-producing regions? Colbert provided the regulatory framework, but the architects of manufacturing growth were the Estates of Languedoc, acting as investors, planners and regulators throughout the eighteenth century. The thesis follows the flows from the receipts of the Treasurer of the Bourse to the strategies of the Castanier and Flottes families and the Pennautier manufactory.

Plastic pollution: the illusion of the circular economy?

Postdoctoral position, ANR PRICk programme — FRAMESPA, University of Perpignan Via Domitia

The genealogy of the consensus behind French and European plastics regulation since 1970: which coalitions of industrialists, experts and policymakers built it, and how. The programme brings together historians, sociologists, lawyers and economists. I build the archival corpus (French National Archives, labour archives, Historical Archives of the European Union in Florence), supplemented by press sources and interviews. The question is the one running through my early modern work: how economic actors take up, negotiate or work around a regulatory framework.

    “Coordinating flows: entrepreneurship and industrial organization in the Pennautier royal manufacture (18th century)”
    Business and Industrial History: A Journal of Archival Research (Glasgow)
    Michael Moss Prize 2026
    Abstract

    Drawing on the private records of the Pennautier royal manufactory, the article questions the notion of the entrepreneur in the early modern period. Entrepreneurial activity appears as the capacity to coordinate heterogeneous flows (capital, raw materials, labour, exports) within a constraining institutional environment.

Organisation of scientific events

MSH Aix-en-Provence

À qui profite le crédit ? Approches sociales de la relation de crédit en Europe (XVIIe–XVIIIe siècles)

Workshop — co-organised with researchers from Aix-Marseille University and the University of Perpignan Via Domitia

The workshop brought together doctoral students and researchers around the social, cultural and economic dimensions of credit relations in Old Regime Europe: who profits from them, what power relations they establish, what effects they have on social structures.
